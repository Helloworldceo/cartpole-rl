How this works

Environment: the classic CartPole-v1 setup — cart mass 1.0 kg, pole mass 0.1 kg, pole half-length 0.5 m, ±10 N bang-bang force, 0.02 s timestep, episode ends if the cart leaves ±2.4 m or the pole tips past ±12°, reward is +1 per surviving step, capped at 500 steps.

Discretization: Q-learning needs a finite table, so each of the 4 continuous state variables is binned into buckets (sliders above). The 4 bin indices combine into one integer "box" — this is the classic Barto–Sutton–Anderson approach to making cart-pole tractable for a lookup-table method.

Update rule: two on-offer. Q-learning (off-policy) — Q(s,a) += α · (r + γ · max Q(s′,·) − Q(s,a)) — always bootstraps off the best action it currently believes is available in the next state, even though ε-greedy exploration means it won't always actually take that action. SARSA (on-policy) — Q(s,a) += α · (r + γ · Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a)) — bootstraps off whatever action it actually selects next (including random exploratory ones), so it learns the value of the policy it's really following, exploration and all. In practice SARSA tends to learn a slightly more "cautious" policy since it accounts for its own chance of exploring into a bad state.

Policy map: for a fixed cart position and velocity (the middle bin of each), it colors every angle/angular-velocity combination by which action the agent currently prefers there. Early in training this is mostly dark (unvisited); as training progresses you should see it resolve into a clean left/right pattern that looks a lot like "push whichever way stops it tipping further" — the policy an engineer would have hand-designed, discovered from scratch by trial and error.

Try this: train 20,000 episodes and Watch — the learning curve is noisy but trends up, and the agent should hold the pole for long stretches. Note how many episodes that took compared to LQR's instant, zero-trial solution in the Control Lab — that gap is the real cost of not having a model of the physics. Then drop x/ẋ bins to 1 and retrain: same episode budget, visibly worse ceiling, because the agent can no longer perceive drift toward the track edge. Or train the same episode budget under Q-learning vs. SARSA and compare their learning curves and final policy maps directly.
